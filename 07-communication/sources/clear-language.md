## 2. Where they agree

- Definition by reader outcome (find, understand, use): L3, L9, L11.
- Most important first: L5, L12, L32, L43, L44, L27.
- One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph: L15, L17, L41, L42.
- Headings and lists are for scanning, not decoration: L42, L33, L24, L6, L41.
- Active voice: L18, L28, L41.
- Everyday words; technical terms allowed if defined, jargon is not: L19, L35, L41, L45.
- Experts also prefer plain language: L30, L45, L37.
- Start from the reader's task: L4, L34, L14, L13.
- Test with real readers rather than trust a formula: L7, L8, L25, L47, L48, L38, L40.

## 3. Where they differ

- Sentence length: 25 words (L29, L35, L41) versus 15-20 (L45); ISO and Digital.gov give no number (L15, L27).
- Paragraph length: 150 words / 3-8 sentences, never more than 250 (L16) versus 1-2 sentence paragraphs (L45).
- Reading level: reading age 9 (L37, GOV.UK original L36 Unknown); adults 9-11 (L38); lower secondary, 7-9 years of school (L40); 8th grade for consumers and 10-12th for experts (L45, L46); ISO and Digital.gov set no level and warn against writing "for an 8th-grade class" without looking at the audience (L26).
- Readability formulas: ISO downplays them (L7); Effortmark rejects them (L47); WCAG uses them to make the criterion testable (L40); GOV.UK recommends Hemingway for active voice, not scoring (L31); Scotland prefers SMOG over Flesch-Kincaid (L38); NN/g uses them as an approximation (L46); IHS says they "should not be your only evaluation tool" (L48).
- Question headings: Digital.gov allows them (L23); GOV.UK discourages them (L33).
- Accessibility scope: ISO 24495-1 excludes it (L2); WCAG 3.1.5 is AAA only (L39).

## 5. What they don't cover (AI output)

- Nothing on machine-generated text: neither default verbosity, nor hedging fillers ("It's important to note..."), nor preambles that push the answer down the page.
- Nothing on conversational format: all of them assume a document or web page; none says how to front-load a one-turn reply or when headings are excessive.
- Nothing on how to say "I don't know" in plain terms, or on citing sources without breaking the sentence-length rule.
- Readability formulas are treated as a human editor's tool; none discusses scoring replies one by one, or the formulas' known failure with lists and fragments (L47, L48), which is the shape of almost all LLM output.
- Nothing on inferring the audience from a single prompt.
- Nothing on cross-language consistency; the 25-word rule is explicitly for English (L41).
- Defining terms assumes a whole document with a glossary at the end (L19); nothing on defining a term once across a conversation.
