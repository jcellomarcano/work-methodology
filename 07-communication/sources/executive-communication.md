## 2. Convergent principles

- Answer or conclusion first: E1, E3, E6, E9, E14, E43, E40.
- A single quick reading must be enough: E2, E33, E35, E36, E43.
- Fewer words, no redundancy: E34, E38, E7, E8, E24, E26.
- Group and layer into chunks: E29, E30, E31, E10, E11, E12, E16, E42, E41.
- Keep what is read together adjacent (avoid split attention): E23, E34, E6.
- Front-load every unit, not just the document: E40, E3, E14.
- Summary first, detail on demand: E46, E47, E43, E11.

## 3. Tensions

- Miller's 7 ± 2 versus Cowan's ~4: Cowan states "only three to five chunks" (E30) and that the seven "was meant more as a rough estimate and a rhetorical device" (E32). Minto cites the seven (E17) and recommends three, ideally one: practice converges with Cowan.
- BLUF versus narrative memos: AR 25-50 requires the main point "at the beginning" (E1); Amazon uses "narratively structured six-page memos" read in "study hall" (E44) that take "a week or more" (E45). Amazon optimizes deep deliberation with committed time; BLUF optimizes fast triage. Neither source accounts for the other.
- Minto's SCQA versus pure BLUF: SCQA opens with situation and complication before the answer (E15); AR 25-50 and Tongue and Quill open with the answer (E1, E6).
- The reading percentages come from web pages (E36, E37 are 2008 eyetracking), not chat or memos.
- Cognitive load theory measures learning, not decision speed (E23-E26); applying it to "act faster on a message" is an inference.
- Progressive disclosure is design guidance, not a controlled study with an effect size (E47).

## 5. Implications for an assistant's response format

1. Open with the answer or recommendation in the first sentence (E1, E3, E14, E43); if there is a question, answer it up top (E6).
2. Design for a fast read and early exit: the reader stops at any point and already has the essentials (E2, E43, E36).
3. Cap of 3-5 top-level items (E30, E31; consistent with E17); the rest, nested (E10-E12, E29).
4. MECE groupings, same kind within each group (E12, E16).
5. Front-load every heading and bullet with the words that carry information (E40); meaningful headings, not clever ones (E34).
6. Cut words and don't repeat the same content twice (E34, E38, E24).
7. Keep dependent pieces adjacent: code and explanation, number and unit, claim and source (E23).
8. Layer: bottom line, 3-5 points, optional detail (E46, E47, E43, E11).
9. SCQA only when the reader has no context (E15); if they do, go straight to BLUF (E1, E5).
10. Short, scannable structure by default; long narrative only when the reader has committed time (E44, E45).
11. The bottom-line block fits on one screen: "24 lines or less" (E7), the 111-word threshold (E38).
