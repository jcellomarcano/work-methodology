The split-attention effect is the extra load a reader carries from having to join two separate sources (say, text and a diagram) in their head to understand something; for technical documentation, the implication is to integrate those sources into a single block instead of keeping them apart.

- What it is: when two sources of information say nothing complete on their own, the learner has to integrate them mentally, and that integration raises cognitive load (the mental effort that occupies working memory) and hampers learning.
- Simile: it's like a recipe with the ingredients on one page and the steps on another, the list is one source, the steps the other, and you have to go back and forth to cook. Unlike the recipe, a technical diagram almost never needs that separation, which is why it's worth avoiding.
- What was measured: presenting only the diagram worked better than presenting the diagram and text together, the extra source didn't add anything, it got in the way (redundancy effect).
- What that load depends on: it isn't the topic that's hard, it's how you present the information and what you ask the reader to do with it (extraneous load).
- For your documentation: put the labels and the explanation inside the diagram; if the diagram already shows something, don't repeat it separately in a paragraph of text.

Next step: review a diagram in your documentation and check whether the accompanying text repeats what the diagram already shows; if it does, move it into the diagram or delete it.
