The "split-attention effect" happens when a reader has to integrate two sources of information to understand something; that integration drains mental resources and hurts learning.

In technical documentation, this means an image accompanied by explanatory text often teaches worse than the image alone. The reader doesn't process both at once; they try to, spend resources coordinating them, and the text competes with the image instead of helping.

What matters is how you present the information and what you ask the reader to do. An annotated screenshot carries less load than that same screenshot plus a paragraph underneath. Sequential steps carry less load than the steps plus a final diagram.

The practical rule: if the text repeats what the image already shows, one of the two has to go. If the steps are clear on their own, remove the image that shows the final result.

Next step: search your documents for where you present duplicated or visually competing information. That's where the effect hurts learning.
