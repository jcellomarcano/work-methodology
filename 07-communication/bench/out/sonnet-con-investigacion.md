The "split-attention effect" is the mental cost of joining two separate sources of information, like text and a diagram, to understand something.

- **What it is:** when you have to mentally integrate two sources to understand the solution, cognitive load goes up (the mental effort that occupies working memory). That hampers learning.
- **Simile:** it's like assembling furniture with the instructions on one sheet and the parts diagram on another. You have to look at the diagram, memorize which piece it is, search for it in the text, and come back, instead of reading it all together. The text is the instructions; the diagram, the parts drawing.
- **Where the simile breaks:** unlike furniture, in a technical document the reader can't always pause. If the text and the diagram are on different pages or columns, every jump costs working memory.
- **Contrast (same source):** separating doesn't help, it can make things worse. Showing only the diagram, without the text set apart, gave a better result than showing both together but separate.
- **What can be controlled:** the part of the load that depends on design can be reduced. It's determined by how the information is presented and what the reader has to do with it.

For writing technical documentation: put the labels and explanations inside the diagram, not in a separate paragraph. If the text sits far from the diagram, it's harder to understand. If an explanation is already clear in the diagram, don't repeat it in separate text.
