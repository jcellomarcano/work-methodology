## The Split-Attention Effect in Technical Documentation

The **split-attention effect** is a cognitive phenomenon that occurs when a reader must integrate information from multiple sources simultaneously to understand a concept. According to Sweller, van Merriënboer and Paas (2019), "Learners must mentally integrate two sources of information in order to understand the solution, a process that yields a high cognitive load and hampers learning." In other words, when you force your reader to coordinate between a diagram and explanatory text at the same time, you overload their processing capacity, hampering learning.

### Implications for technical documentation

The most direct consequence is that you must avoid unnecessary redundancy. The same research shows that "only presenting the diagram was superior to presenting both sources of information together." This means that if a diagram clearly communicates an idea, adding redundant text that repeats it visually hurts more than it helps.

What *does* matter is how you structure the information. As these authors note, extraneous load, the load that depends on instructional design, is determined by "how the information is presented and what the learner is required to do by the instructional procedure." In practice: choose between clear text *or* a clean diagram (not both at once), order the steps logically, and design the reading flow to be intuitive.
