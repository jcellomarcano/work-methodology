Room, not SharedPreferences and not a custom WAL. Let me explain why it fits your priority order (Money > Payment state > Recoverability > UX):

**Why not SharedPreferences:** it gives you neither transactionality nor atomicity. An `apply()`/`commit()` can end up half-done if the process dies at the wrong moment, and you have no way to record a sequence of events (requested → sent to provider → confirmed). With money involved, that's exactly what you can't afford.

**Why not a custom WAL:** Room already runs on SQLite in WAL mode. Hand-reimplementing a write-ahead log duplicates, worse, what you already have, and adds a new surface of bugs right in the most sensitive part of the system, without gaining anything Room doesn't already give you.

**How to set it up with Room:**

1. A `PaymentIntent` entity with an idempotent `id` (generated by you before calling the provider, not by it) and a `state` field with an explicit state machine: `CREATED` → `SENT_TO_PROVIDER` → `CONFIRMED` / `FAILED` / `UNKNOWN`.
2. Before calling the provider, write (in a transaction) the record in `SENT_TO_PROVIDER` with a timestamp and the idempotent `id`. That write commits to disk before making the network call.
3. If the process dies between the call and the response, on restart look for records in `SENT_TO_PROVIDER` and reconcile them against the provider using the idempotent `id` (status query or idempotent retry), never resending the payment blindly.
4. The provider's response updates the record to `CONFIRMED` or `FAILED` in another transaction. If after reconciliation you still don't know the outcome, mark it `UNKNOWN` and treat it as a case to resolve explicitly (neither success nor failure).

This covers Money (ACID transactions, no partial writes), Payment state (a state machine with evidence persisted at every transition), and Recoverability (you can reconstruct exactly what happened after the process died). UX gets built on top of this state, not the other way around.

If synchronous I/O performance on the thread that calls the provider worries you, use `runInTransaction` with the minimum writes needed and keep the rest (logs, analytics) off the critical path.
