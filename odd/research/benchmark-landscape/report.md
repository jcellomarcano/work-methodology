# Benchmark recall, cost and activation, not ceremony

The strengths of an AI coding-agent harness that are worth benchmarking are the ones with outcome-level evidence behind them: run-to-run reliability (pass^k), independent verification measured as defect recall against ground truth, cost per solved task, and whether the harness's mechanisms actually fire. Rules files, memory, routing and spec ceremony have null, proxy-only or vendor-only evidence. **No public metric has been independently shown to predict real-world value**: the only "offline predicts production" claim is GitHub's own, about its own reviewer. For the work-methodology kit (github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology), the comparison with roughly fifteen frameworks shows that most of its workflow is commodity. Spec documents, TDD prompting, hooks, subagent model fields, memory and multi-agent review are everywhere. Its frozen-tree review receipts and risk tiers repeat Gentle AI's RDD, from which the kit's ODD/RDD were adapted. Two things are unclaimed elsewhere in the survey: per-claim epistemic labels with a citation-existence check, and determinism tests on the kit's own scripts. A third is rare: a self-benchmark with a vanilla arm, arm-validity gates and published "unsupported" verdicts. The kit's own data, all self-reported and mostly n=1, supports its blocking gates and its two adversaries. It does not support its warn-only lints, Engram memory, router, judge filtering, receipts used as a gate, or a claim that its review beats native tooling on value. Independent harness studies find near-zero average effects between mature harnesses, with run-to-run swings of 2 to 6 points. A credible kit-vs-vanilla-vs-native benchmark therefore has to compute pass^k, paired confidence intervals, seeded-defect recall, cost per solved task and activation itself. It should take tokens, cost, effort, subagent attribution and compaction from Claude Code's OpenTelemetry instead of rebuilding them.

**Reading conventions.** Every claim carries a label: Measured (a number from a study, telemetry or a file), Tested (a result of a test or a verifier), Inferred (reasoning from cited facts), Assumed (unverified) or Unknown. **[Vendor-run]** marks a number produced by the seller of the thing measured, or by a benchmark owner scoring its own product. **[Self-reported]** marks every kit number: the kit's author produced them with the kit's own scripts, and nobody has replicated them. **(PK)** marks findings that the research team took from prior knowledge of the primary source and did not re-fetch on 2026-10-20; spot-check them before quoting. The research notes give dates as publication dates where available, otherwise as "accessed 2026-10-20". Kit citations point to the public repo at commit `a22062b` unless they are marked local. Local files are not on `main`.

## Noise of 2 to 6 points sets the bar every harness claim must clear

The best-evidenced fact about agent harnesses concerns measurement itself. Bjarnason, Silva and Monperrus analysed **60,000 SWE-bench Verified trajectories**. Picking a different single run moved pass@1 by **2.2 to 6.0 percentage points**, and the standard deviation stayed **above 1.5 pp even at temperature 0** [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2602.07150, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150)). A vendor blog saw one model score 70.6% and then 72.8%, with 53 tasks flipping verdict between the two runs [Measured, vendor blog, not peer-reviewed] ([LayerLens](https://layerlens.ai/blog/the-test-your-agent-never-took)). τ-bench introduced pass^k, the probability that all k trials succeed. GPT-4o agents succeeded on fewer than half the tasks, and pass^8 fell **below 25%** in retail [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2406.12045, 2024-06-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)). Anthropic's eval guidance gives a worked example: 75% per-trial success is only about 42% pass^3 [Vendor guidance] ([Anthropic, 2026-01-09](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). The kit's existing vanilla comparisons are all n=1 per cell [Measured, self-reported], so on this evidence none of them can separate a harness effect from noise [Inferred].

The second strong body of evidence is about where value leaks: verification. Faros telemetry covers more than 10,000 developers on 1,255 teams. High-adoption teams completed 21% more tasks and merged 98% more PRs, while review time rose **91%**, PR size **154%** and bugs per developer 9%. The link to company-level metrics "evaporates" [Measured, vendor telemetry, correlational; 2025 date (PK)] ([Faros research](https://faros.ai/research); [Faros blog](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering)). DORA reported in March 2026 that time saved in creation is "frequently re-allocated to auditing and verification" [Measured summary, independent] ([DORA, 2026-03](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/)). DORA 2025 found AI now associated with higher throughput but still with higher instability [Measured, secondary coverage] ([RedMonk, 2025-12-18](https://redmonk.com/rstephens/2025/12/18/dora2025/)). Self-correction does not fill the gap. Without external feedback, intrinsic self-correction does not improve reasoning and can degrade it [Measured, independent, (PK)] ([arXiv 2310.01798, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). LLM self-recognition also correlates linearly with self-preference [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2404.13076, 2024-04-15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)). There is counter-evidence: a June 2026 preprint finds self-preference weak or absent when a deterministic verifier is present, but on text, not code ([arXiv 2606.20093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20093), summary only). On verifiable tasks, part of a model's self-preference reflects genuinely better outputs ([Chen et al. 2025, listing](https://papers.lunadong.com/paper/6021)).

Review benchmarks show that **recall, not precision, separates reviewers**. On a ReviewBench capture dated 2026-10-19, grounded precision spans only **83.6% to 90.2%** across 33 rows, while grounded recall spans **5.7% to 46.3%** [Secondary aggregator] ([benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/reviewbench)). Configuration alone moves recall. Klaussy on the same Sonnet 5.5 model goes from 22.6% to 29.8% with nits switched on, and Codex GPT-5.6 Sol goes from 9.7% at Low effort to 18.5% at Max [Secondary]. SWE-PRBench uses 350 PRs with human review comments as ground truth. No model detects more than **31%** of human-flagged issues, and all 8 models **degrade monotonically as context grows**. A 2,000-token diff-with-summary prompt beats a 2,500-token full-context prompt [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2603.26130, 2026-03](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26130v1)).

Context discipline has strong evidence of harm from bloat and weak evidence of gains from management. Chroma's "Context Rot" study tested 18 LLMs and found that performance falls as input length grows, even on simple tasks [Measured, (PK)] ([Chroma, 2025-07-14](https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot)). The ETH Zurich and LogicStar study of AGENTS.md is the strongest study of rules files. Context files do not raise task success, they increase inference cost by **more than 20%**, and repository overviews "are not helpful" [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2602.11988, v3 2026-09-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)). A smaller study of 124 PRs found that AGENTS.md lowered runtime and output tokens with "comparable task completion" [Measured, independent; the ~28.6% and ~16.6% magnitudes are (PK)] ([arXiv 2601.20404, 2026-01](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404)). Anthropic reports context editing at **+29%** and memory plus editing at **+39%**, with **84% fewer tokens**, but on internal agentic-search evals, not coding [Measured, vendor-run] ([Anthropic, 2025-09-29](https://www.anthropic.com/news/context-management)). SkillsBench gives the most useful positive signal. Curated, focused skills added **+16.2 pp** on average, only about +4.5 pp in software engineering, while self-generated skills added nothing (the NeurIPS version reports −1.3 pp) [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2602.12670](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v1)).

Delegation helps or hurts depending on task structure. A Google Research and MIT study covered 180 configurations. Centralized multi-agent coordination added **80.9%** on parallelizable tasks, but "for sequential reasoning, all multi-agent variants degraded performance by **39-70%**" [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2512.08296, 2025-12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)). Anthropic's research system beat a single agent by 90.2% at about 15× chat tokens, and Anthropic itself names "most coding tasks" as less suited [Measured, vendor-run, (PK)] ([Anthropic, 2025-06-13](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). The two results reconcile once task type is controlled. They point to delegating read-only exploration and verification, not interdependent writes [Inferred].

The remaining strengths are weak. For **memory**, a pre-registered SWE-bench Verified test with Claude Code went from 67.3% to 77.6% on one trial, but the multi-seed 95% CI has a lower bound of zero [Measured, independent, unreviewed, n=49] ([GitHub](https://github.com/SaravananJaichandar/coding-agent-memory-benchmark)). VibeMemBench keeps only targets where memory already helped, which inflates the effect by construction ([arXiv 2609.23570, 2026-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23570)). For **routing**, RouteLLM's 35% to 85% cost cuts at 95% of GPT-4 quality are on chat benchmarks [Measured, independent, (PK)] ([LMSYS, 2024-07-01](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/)). Opus 4.5 at medium effort matched Sonnet 4.5's best SWE-bench score with 76% fewer output tokens [Measured, vendor-run, (PK) for wording] ([Anthropic, 2025-11-24](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-5)). Aider's architect/editor split added 2.7 to 5.3 pp on its own benchmark [Measured, vendor-run] ([aider, 2024-09-26](https://aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html)). No independent study of routing inside a coding harness at fixed quality exists [Unknown]. For **rules conformance**, instruction-following degrades as rule count grows, with the best models near 68% at 500 instructions [Measured, (PK)] ([IFScale, arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)). No controlled study compares hook-enforced rules with prompted rules [Unknown].

For **delivery speed**, METR's 2025 RCT (16 developers, 246 tasks) measured AI-allowed tasks taking **19% longer** (CI +2% to +39%), while developers believed they were 20% faster [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2507.09089, 2025-07](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)). METR's 2026-02-24 update reports −18% for returning developers (CI −38% to +9%) and −4% for new ones (CI −15% to +9%). METR calls this "only very weak evidence" because 30% to 50% of tasks were withheld by developers who did not want to work without AI [Measured, independent] ([METR, 2026-02-24](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)).

| Strength | Best evidence (producer) | Evidence level | Benchmark it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reliability | 2.2–6.0 pp single-run swing; pass^8 <25% (independent) | outcome, strong | pass^k, flip rate — a precondition for everything else |
| Independent verification | review time +91%, instability up (vendor telemetry, DORA); recall 5.7–46.3% vs flat precision | outcome, strong but correlational | seeded-defect recall by severity, defect escape |
| Context discipline | Context Rot (PK); AGENTS.md +20% cost, no gain (independent) | outcome on cost, strong | cost per solved task, peak context |
| Delegation topology | +80.9% parallel / −39–70% sequential (independent) | outcome, task-dependent | success and cost split by task structure |
| Rules files / conformance | null on success, +20% cost; enforced vs prompted Unknown | outcome null; gap | violation rate, warn vs block |
| Activation / adoption | METR selection bias; kit "installed ≈ vanilla" (self-reported) | gate on all others | share of eligible tasks where the mechanism ran |
| Routing / effort | vendor proxies only | proxy | cost at equal quality |
| Memory | +10 pp single trial, CI touches 0 | weakest | resume-task success, multi-seed |
| Delivery speed | METR RCTs, CIs cross zero | outcome, weak at small n | measured wall-clock, never self-report |

Which metrics predict real value has a short honest answer. **Self-reported speedup, PR or task throughput and lines of code have documented ways to mislead**: METR's perception gap, and PR size growing alongside throughput [Inferred from the cited studies]. The only claim that an offline benchmark predicted a production outcome is GitHub's own. It says ReviewBench "consistently pointed in the same direction" as production, and that its online A/B cut cost per review by 8.0% [Vendor-run] ([GitHub, 2026-10-05](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/reviewbench-an-open-benchmark-for-ai-code-review/)). No independent study links any benchmark score to merge rate or defect escape on real teams [Unknown]. The defensible choice is to measure quantities that are outcomes in their own right, such as defects found against ground truth, cost per verified solution and reliability across trials, instead of proxies hoped to predict them.

## Harness swings of 8 to 34 points shrink to near zero between mature harnesses

How large an effect should a kit-vs-vanilla benchmark expect? Zhang et al. compiled leaderboard evidence: Claude Opus 4.5 scored **45.9% under Scale's SEAL scaffold and 55.4% under Claude Code** on SWE-bench Pro, and on HAL's Verified Mini, scaffold choice spans 34 pp for Sonnet 4.5. Their controlled factorial (3 models × 3 harnesses, 100 tasks, 2 runs per cell) found **harness variance 7.80 times model variance**, with six ranking reversals in nine comparisons; the authors note that 7.80× "is not universal" [Measured, independent; leaderboard figures are secondary within the paper] ([arXiv 2605.23950, 2026-05-07](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950v1)). Those swings compare minimal and full harnesses. Arjmandi compared two mature harnesses on a contamination-controlled private suite. For Opus 4.8, the native and neutral harnesses differed by **−1.25 pp, CI [−10.0, +7.5]**, and "neither contrast resolves an average advantage." A post-hoc split shows −9.0 pp on repo tasks and +23.7 pp on contest tasks, which the author says needs designed replication. The neutral harness cost **1.3 to 1.6 times more per solved task**, and **22 of 81 runs cancelled at the wall-clock ceiling had already produced a passing patch** [Measured, independent, single author] ([arXiv 2609.11987, 2026-09-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11987)).

Kit versus vanilla is a mature-versus-mature comparison: same CLI, same model, with and without a methodology layer. The prior from these studies is therefore a small average success effect, possibly opposite effects across task types, and **cost as the most sensitive signal** [Inferred]. Arjmandi's paired CIs of roughly ±6 to ±9 pp on 80 tasks imply that the kit's pre-registered Phase 2 (10 tasks × 3 arms × 3 reps = 90 runs; [GH/08-benchmark/protocols/phase2-protocol.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/protocols/phase2-protocol.md)) can resolve only very large success-rate differences. This is a power assumption that nobody has verified [Assumed]. Grader error is a second constraint. DeepSWE found that its LLM judge disagreed with SWE-bench Pro's inherited tests on **32.4%** of trials, against 1.4% for its own behaviour-level verifiers. In 33 of 38 cheating trials (87%), the agent read the gold commit out of `.git` history [Measured, independent] ([arXiv 2607.07946, 2026-07-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07946)). OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified on 2026-02-23, citing contamination and flawed tests [Measured, vendor audit; quote from search snippet] ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)). A private-repo benchmark avoids contamination only if it strips history and uses verifiers that accept any correct implementation [Inferred].

## Most of the kit duplicates commodity or Gentle AI mechanisms; three things are rare

The landscape converges on one shape: instructions file, spec, task list, implementation, review. Almost all of it is enforced by prompt. Code enforcement comes from host runtimes: Claude Code's 33 hook events, where "exit 2 blocks" ([Claude Code hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks), accessed 2026-10-20); Kiro's `PreToolUse` hooks and property-based tests ([Kiro docs, updated 2026-10-10 and 2026-08-04](https://kiro.dev/docs/hooks/)); and Cursor's Plan Mode, whose enforcement is disputed on the vendor's own forum ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/plan-mode-is-not-respected-by-the-agent/151802)) [Measured]. Spec Kit (142.1k stars), BMAD (54.4k), Superpowers (~302.4k) and Agent OS publish no measurement of their own effect. Superpowers ships an eval harness with no published results [Measured, accessed 2026-10-20] ([spec-kit](https://github.com/github/spec-kit); [BMAD](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD); [superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers)). The independent critiques of heavy methodology have numbers in only one case. A single Scott Logic run took about 5.5 hours of review and about 4.8k Markdown lines for two Spec Kit features. Iterative prompting took 8 minutes of agent time plus 24 minutes of review, with "no qualitative benefit"; the article's per-phase numbers do not sum to its stated totals [Measured, single practitioner] ([Scott Logic, 2025-11-26](https://blog.scottlogic.com/2025/11/26/putting-spec-kit-through-its-paces-radical-idea-or-reinvented-waterfall.html)). Thoughtworks put spec-driven development in "Assess" on 2025-11-05 and dropped it from Radar Vol 34 in April 2026 ([Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-au/radar/techniques/spec-driven-development)). Vendors responded with proportionality: Kiro Quick Spec, BMAD's "Small changes go straight to build", and Gentle AI's sizing "never by file count" [Inferred from features].

The kit describes itself as "Scripts supply evidence, tests supply guarantees, agents supply reasoning, humans supply judgment" ([GH/README.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/README.md)). Set against that landscape, it lands as follows.

**Public benchmarks answer a different question from the kit's.** The SWE-bench family, Terminal-Bench and Aider polyglot score a model and scaffold together on fixed public tasks with hidden tests. Of the major boards, only Aider holds the harness fixed and reports dollar cost: gpt-5 (high) at 88.0% for $29.08 [Measured, Aider-run, updated 2025-11-20] ([aider leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)). τ-bench contributes the pass^k metric. METR's time horizon needs human time baselines that a small team cannot build. ReviewBench and SWE-PRBench measure review against gold sets, and the kit can reuse their definitions directly: grounded precision and recall with severity slices, detection rate and false-positive rate. ReviewBench's own design carries two warnings. It publishes a score only when it beats the submitter's previous best, a best-of policy, and a third-party (AI-authored) critique says 45.2% of its gold true positives came from Copilot code review producers, the owner's product [Secondary] ([GitHub, 2026-10-05](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/reviewbench-an-open-benchmark-for-ai-code-review/); [issue #282](https://github.com/JPHutchins/code-review/issues/282)). The kit's gold set must therefore come from seeded defects or post-merge fixes, never from any reviewer under test [Inferred]. None of these benchmarks measures a methodology layer on the user's own repository with and without the layer installed, which is the kit's actual question [Inferred].

**Observability tools already compute most of the telemetry layer.** Claude Code's OpenTelemetry export provides per-request tokens split into input, output, cacheRead and cacheCreation; `cost_usd`, which the docs call "approximations"; `duration_ms`; `model`; `effort`; `query_source` (main/subagent/auxiliary); `agent.name`, `skill.name` and `plugin.name`; `tool_result` success and duration; and a `claude_code.compaction` event with `pre_tokens` and `post_tokens` [Measured, accessed 2026-10-20] ([Claude Code monitoring](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage)). ccusage aggregates local JSONL by session, day or 5-hour block, with cache tokens kept separate and prices recomputed from LiteLLM [Measured] ([ccusage](https://github.com/ryoppippi/ccusage)). Langfuse captures Claude Code traces through the Stop hook and runs dataset experiments [Measured, vendor docs via search snippet] ([Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/integrations/other/claude-code)). Braintrust, Phoenix, LangSmith and Weave are described as evaluation or observability platforms, but only through third-party comparison articles [weak, secondary] ([MarkTechPost, 2026-08-09](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/09/top-llm-observability-and-evaluation-platforms-in-2026-langfuse-langsmith-braintrust-arize-and-more-compared/)). **None is documented to run a coding agent end to end against a task suite and compare two harness configurations** [Inferred]. The kit's transcript extractors duplicate OTel and ccusage for tokens and cost. They add kit-specific counters that no tool provides: hook runs, gate decisions, receipt verdicts, tag shares and arm validity [Inferred].

## The kit's own numbers support gates and adversaries, not its review economics

The kit is unusually candid: it publishes tables that mark its own claims unsupported. Read together, its evidence follows one pattern. **Mechanisms that are code-enforced and blocking show an effect; warn-only and prompt-only mechanisms do not** [Inferred, consistent with the author's own reading]. All figures in this section are [Measured, self-reported] unless labelled otherwise, mostly from one owner's repositories and mostly from one Android/Kotlin project.

Supported, with caveats: the gates fired, with 1 force-push denied, 18 merges and 10 forbidden-path writes denied, and 135 budget denials, though "precision never measured" ([phase1-findings.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/reports/phase1-findings.md) §3–4). Delegation share rose from 0.16 to 0.29. Commit hygiene rose from 0.35 to 0.88 for Conventional Commits and from 0.38 to 0.73 for code commits with tests, but in one project only, and another project fell from 0.98 to 0.53. Peak parent context fell from 353k to 257k, but over the same period "3,304 of 5,917 requests with a recorded effort ran at `medium`", which the author lists as confounder #1 (same file, §0–1). The strongest positive signal comes from field review rounds (local `08-benchmark/reports/field-2026-10-14-17.md` §2, not on `main`). "11 of 17 R1 rows came only from the adversaries." The challenger and the blind-spot adversary overlapped on about 1 item in 20. A second round found that first-round fixes had broken two rulings. All of this is static reading with no runtime confirmation and n=3 rounds. It fits the independent evidence that external verification beats self-correction [Inferred].

Unsupported by the kit's own data: warn-only lints produced 439 warnings with no fix evidence. Engram had "405 Engram sessions, 373 with zero observations (92%)" and a recall median of 0. The judge refuted 3 of about 58 rulings, so it "merges and grades" more than it filters. A high-tier receipt was left `started` with 0 captures while the change was force-pushed, so the gate "is advisory in practice". The coordinator-only rule broke twice in one session. Evidence tags appeared in only "2 of 94 replies" where no lint checked them. The router is at evidence rung 0, with "no logged decision" (phase1-findings.md §5, §10; local field report §3, §5; local `odd/research/kit-direction/audit.md` §2). Unknown: defect recall against ground truth, because Phase 2 has not run. Delivery speed is also Unknown: the lead-time "after" sample is n=3. So is the quality cost of budget denials.

**Activation is the first-order variable.** With a neutral prompt, the installed kit behaved like vanilla on PR 176: USD 1.28 vs 1.34, 9 vs 10 turns, 6 vs 6 valid findings. "In `claude -p` with a neutral prompt, the kit's review workflow does not start on its own" ([GH/08-benchmark/reports/pilot-pr176.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/reports/pilot-pr176.md) §1–2). The 2026-10-18 audit repeated this on a fixture task.

Three integrity gaps cut against the kit's "evidence or silence" rule [Measured, from the research team's read of `a22062b`; the suite was not run]. The README status block says it is "never typed by hand", yet its `measured_at` sha `85bb975` does not exist in the public repo, so the block was measured privately and copied. ## A portable benchmark must compute success, recall and pass^k itself

The benchmark skill has to run in two places: inside the kit, and in a vanilla Claude Code session with no `metodo` MCP server. It should therefore be stdlib scripts that read transcripts and OTel and write a deterministic, row-labelled report. Every number would come from a catalogue row and carry a label, and every script would ship with a determinism test and a negative control. These are the kit's own rules ([GH/08-benchmark/METRICS.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/METRICS.md); [GH/CONTEXT-PACK.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/CONTEXT-PACK.md) §2), and they are the part of the kit most worth carrying over [Inferred]. The skill should have two modes and label them differently. An observational per-session mode reports what happened in a real session, labelled Measured for counts and Inferred for any attribution to the harness. An experimental A/B mode runs the same tasks under different arms, and only there can success differences be attributed to the harness.

**Arms.** The arms should be: (A) vanilla, using the kit's existing recipe `METODO_TOOLS=/nonexistent claude -p --disable-slash-commands --strict-mcp-config`, but with the residual `.claude/agents/generic-*.md` removed or recorded, because they "stay discoverable by the `Agent` tool" ([pr-review-ab.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/protocols/pr-review-ab.md)); (B) kit installed with a neutral prompt, which measures activation; (C) kit invoked; and (D) native tooling, meaning the `code-review` plugin and `/code-review`. Because the kit's ODD/RDD derive from Gentle AI, an optional arm (E) running Gentle AI is the honest comparator for those mechanisms [Inferred recommendation]. Every arm needs a Harness Card covering execution, tools, context, scheduling, observability, verification and governance ([arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950v1)). The kit's `invalid-arm` rule should be kept: a kit run with no subagent, skill or `mcp__metodo__*` call is not a kit run ([phase2-protocol.md](https://github.com/jcellomarcano/work-methodology/blob/a22062b/08-benchmark/protocols/phase2-protocol.md) §6).

**Controls.** Hold the model id and effort fixed, and record the effort per request from OTel `effort`. Use fixed wall-clock ceilings and audit timed-out runs for passing patches; 22 of 81 had one in Arjmandi's study. Start every trial from a clean environment, as Anthropic's guidance requires after Claude read earlier trials' git history ([Anthropic, 2026-01-09](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). Strip `.git` history after the base commit. Record cache state, because cached input is priced at one tenth of uncached input [Measured list prices, (PK)] ([Manus, 2025-07-18](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)). Use 20 to 50 tasks drawn from real failures, which Anthropic's guidance calls "a great start." Pre-register strata: review, fix with a decoy, feature, resume, and sequential versus parallelizable work. Run k ≥ 3 trials per arm per task, and report pass@1, pass^k and paired task-level bootstrap 95% CIs, with permutation tests and no strata chosen after seeing the data ([arXiv 2609.11987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11987)).

**Judges.** Run deterministic checks first, because judges show "significant randomness" on code correctness across 26 models ([CodeJudgeBench, arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10535)) [Measured, independent]. Then use two LLM judges from different model families, blind to the arm. Run every pairwise comparison in both orders, because position bias grows as the quality gap shrinks, exactly when arms are close ([arXiv 2406.07791](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v9)). Give each judge an "Unknown" option and one rubric per dimension. Have a human re-label a sample of at least 30 items and report κ: SWE-PRBench reached κ=0.75 on N=30 and 0.616 across judge models ([arXiv 2603.26130](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26130v1)), and ReviewBench reports 96.6% senior-engineer agreement. The kit's planned Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 judge pair is one family judging that family's output, so it should gain a non-Anthropic judge or rest its headline on deterministic seeded-defect matching [Inferred]. One fingerprint is specific to the kit. Its outputs carry evidence tags and `path:line@sha` citations, which reveal the arm to a "blind" judge and can trigger verbosity bias, so findings should be normalized to a common schema before judging [Inferred].

**What to reuse, what to compute.** Reuse OTel for tokens by type, `cost_usd`, `duration_ms`, `model`, `effort`, `query_source`, `agent.name`/`skill.name`, `tool_result` and the compaction `pre_tokens`/`post_tokens`. Where OTel is absent, parse the transcript JSONL defensively by `version`; it carries `message.usage`, `effort`, `isSidechain` and a `cost-state` record, but the format is undocumented and changes between versions [Measured, local transcripts, Claude Code 2.1.281–2.1.299]. Cross-check cost with ccusage, and state which cost source is the headline, since OTel cost is an estimate and ccusage's is a LiteLLM recomputation. Langfuse or Braintrust are optional storage and visualization, not the evaluator. The skill must compute the rest itself: behaviour-level task success; A/B orchestration with repeats; pass^k and flip rate; paired statistics; cost per solved task; context occupancy (input + cacheRead + cacheCreation per request, divided by a window size from a config table, because neither OTel nor the transcripts state the window); seeded-defect recall by severity; judge scoring with bias controls; activation; gate precision; and timeout audits. No surveyed tool ships any of these for coding-agent harnesses [Inferred].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Success rate (rows 2–3) should be reported as a non-inferiority check with its CI, not as the headline, because a 10-to-50-task design can resolve only large differences [Assumed power]. The headline should be cost per solved task, recall by severity and activation (rows 1, 4, 5), the quantities most likely to differ between mature harnesses at small n [Inferred from arXiv 2609.11987 and ReviewBench's precision/recall spread].

## Conclusion: where the value really is

**The kit's real value is that it measures honestly, not that it orchestrates.** Its workflow layer duplicates commodity mechanisms or Gentle AI's. Its memory, router and warn-only lints have no supporting evidence, even in its own data. Its review loop beats vanilla only when someone invokes it, and costs about 6× more [Measured, self-reported, n=1]. Two things do earn a place. Code-enforced blocking guards are cheap and fire, though their precision is unmeasured. Two independent adversaries cover different ground and catch regressions from first-round fixes, which fits the independent evidence that external verification beats self-correction [Inferred]. What is genuinely rare is the measurement discipline: labelled claims checked against real citations, scripts proven deterministic, arm-validity gates, and a published list of its own unsupported claims. Independent studies find near-zero average effects between mature harnesses, and no framework in the survey has an independent controlled evaluation. An instrument that measures any harness honestly, whether kit, vanilla, native `code-review` or Gentle AI, is therefore worth more than one more harness [Inferred].

This changes the kit's direction more than its marketing. The kit's own data shows that enforced rules held and prompted rules did not [self-reported]. No external controlled study exists on that question [Unknown], so the kit is in a position to produce the field's first evidence on it by running rows 1, 13 and 14 under `warn` vs `block`. Prompt-only rules that cannot be moved into code should be measured once and dropped if they do not fire. The portable benchmark skill should ship before more workflow does, and it should lead with cost per solved task, severity-sliced recall on seeded defects and activation, judged across model families. Until then, every comparative claim the kit makes, positive or negative, stays a signal and not a conclusion. The kit's own reports already say so.
